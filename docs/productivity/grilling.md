## What it does

`grilling` stress-tests a plan through a focused interview. Its stopping point is an actionable requested outcome, with routine choices recorded as assumptions.

## When to reach for it

Type `/grilling`, or a planning workflow can use it when material product decisions remain. Use [grill-me](https://github.com/bandoyer/skills/blob/main/docs/productivity/grill-me.md) for an interview, or [grill-with-docs](https://github.com/bandoyer/skills/blob/main/docs/engineering/grill-with-docs.md) when you also want domain documents maintained.

## Common questions

**Will it ask me to specify every detail?**

No. It asks about decisions that change the deliverable and chooses reasonable defaults for routine details. Accessible facts are the agent's job to look up.

**Can I cap the interview?**

Yes. State a question or round limit; it will preserve the answers and report what remains unresolved when the allowance is reached.

**Does finishing the interview authorize implementation?**

An interview alone does not. If you already requested implementation after planning, the agent can continue once material decisions are settled, unless you reserved a checkpoint.

## It's working if

- Questions explain their consequences and include a recommendation.
- Each round advances the requested outcome.
- Routine assumptions do not become extra approval questions.
- The interview ends without exploring unrelated future features.

## Where it fits

It is the interview method shared by relevant planning tools. [ask-matt](https://github.com/bandoyer/skills/blob/main/docs/engineering/ask-matt.md) helps choose the smallest useful workflow.
